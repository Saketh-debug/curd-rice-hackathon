The LAYAA Decision Engine: Why It Solves Alert Fatigue

Located in server/laya_decision.py, the LAYAA Decision Engine is the arbitration bridge between raw machine learning probabilities and human mission operators.

1. Calibrated Confidence (No Dice Rolls)

Raw ML models produce uncalibrated probabilities that frequently over-score noise. LAYAA maps the 52 network features into calibrated risk tiers:

  • CRITICAL: ≥ 95% Confidence → Instant AUTO_BLOCK
  • HIGH / ELEVATED: 40% – 94% → ESCALATE_TO_ANALYST
  • NOMINAL: < 40% → Quiet MONITOR

2. Sub-Millisecond Feature Explainability

For every escalated packet, LAYAA extracts the top 3 physical telemetry signals driving the score, eliminating the black-box effect:

  • Packet Inter-Arrival: Near zero (<1ms surge)
  • Connection Pattern: Unfinished SYN handshake flood
  • Burst Deviation: 3.4× above baseline nominal flow

Judge Q&A Quick Defense Guide

Q1: "How do you ensure your models don't accidentally block legitimate satellite commands?"

Answer: That is the exact reason we built the LAYAA Decision Engine. Only events exceeding our high-confidence threshold (≥95%) trigger an automated block. Anything borderline in the 40–70% range is elevated to human flight controllers in Portal B with full feature attribution. Furthermore, our Reinforcement Learning loop penalizes false alarms heavily, continuously widening safety margins on critical telecommand channels.

Q2: "Why can't existing enterprise tools (like Splunk or CrowdStrike) do this?"

Answer: Enterprise tools are designed for standard enterprise IT protocols (HTTP, Active Directory, DNS). They have no native understanding of CCSDS frame architectures, Space Link Extension (SLE) ports, CLTU command structures, or the physics of an 8-minute satellite pass window. SOAR Space is purpose-built for the constraints of aerospace telemetry.

Q3: "Is AEGIS sending sensitive ground-station telemetry to OpenAI or an external cloud?"

Answer: No, absolutely not. AEGIS runs completely air-gapped on local hardware utilizing Ollama and Qwen 4B with a local in-memory vector index. It was engineered specifically to adhere to ISRO mission security protocols where zero data may egress the physical Operations Complex.
